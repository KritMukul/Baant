Send it to the table. Link or QR.

The moment after the host taps "Looks right". The bill becomes a link. The host sends it on WhatsApp, copies it, or holds up a QR for the people sitting next to them, then watches friends arrive. Six live states, two themes. The QR is real: it encodes the exact link and the test decodes it.

What this screen decides

  • One primary action: send it. "Send on WhatsApp" opens WhatsApp with the message already written (wa.me works on every phone, no API). Copy, QR and the system share sheet sit one row below.
  • The QR is for the people next to you. It opens full size in a sheet, always dark on white even in dark mode (inverted codes scan badly). It is the same link, not a separate join path.
  • The link is the key. Anyone with it can see the bill and pick items, and the screen says so in one line. Only the host can edit the bill or confirm payments.
  • Nobody has joined, then somebody has. The panel starts empty ("Friends show up here the moment they open the link"), then fills live with who is in and how many items are picked. Once a friend is in, the primary button becomes "Open the board" and "Edit" disappears: editing items under people who already picked is done from the board.
  • A bill shared short stays honest. If the host used "Share anyway" on the last screen, a note says how much short it is and offers a way back.
  • Failure keeps the work. If the link cannot be created, the bill stays as it was and "Try again" is the only action. The build must keep the draft on the phone until the link exists.

Three things to approve

  • WhatsApp first. The primary button names WhatsApp because that is where Indian groups live; the system share sheet is under "More" for everything else. The alternative is one generic "Share" button that always opens the system sheet (one extra tap for WhatsApp).
  • Joining is live and visible to the host (who opened the link, how many items are picked), and "Edit" is hidden once the first friend is in.
  • The link looks like krit.website/b/k3Fq9aB: seven characters, case-sensitive, as the app already makes them. They are hard to read aloud, which is why the QR and the share button exist. The payment reference (7K2) is a different, shorter code that goes in the UPI note.

Next after approval: Join (guest, first open of the link), then Settings. Then tablet and desktop, where the QR becomes the main way to bring phones in.
