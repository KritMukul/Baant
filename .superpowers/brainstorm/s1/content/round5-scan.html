Dibs · Step 5 · Screen 5 of 9 · Phone · Host

Check the scan. Fix it fast.

Right after Scan, before anyone sees the link. The host compares what Dibs read with the bill and fixes anything wrong. Six live states built on real bills: edit an item, add the tax the scan missed, remove a duplicate, review the unclear reads.

What this screen decides

  • One test decides everything: items + charges against the bill's own total. It is the same validateBill the server uses (₹1 tolerance), ported and checked against the shared vectors. Green means the numbers add up, not that every word was read right.
  • A difference is never just a number. "₹52.22 short" comes with what is probably wrong and a one-tap fix when the scan itself saw the missing line ("Found on the bill: S.Tax ₹51.75. Add it?").
  • Unclear reads are marked where they happen. A warning edge and a "Check" tag on the row, with what was read and why it was doubtful. They never block sharing; the totals test does.
  • Round-off is automatic and visible. If the bill's own arithmetic is off by under ₹1 (the Local Diners bill is ₹0.13 over), Dibs records a round-off so friends pay exactly the printed total. It says so.
  • Prices are before tax. Tax-inclusive line totals were the bug that made Liquor Street ₹52 off, so the screen says it once at the top of the list.
  • Sharing anyway is possible but not easy. It asks once and says what it means: friends will pay a little less than the bill.

Three things to approve

  • Check-the-scan is a required stop, not a skippable step. Even when the scan matches, the host sees it once. It costs one tap and prevents a wrong bill reaching four friends.
  • Round-off is added automatically up to ₹1 and shown. Above ₹1 it needs a fix or an explicit "Share anyway".
  • Editing is per item through a sheet, not inline fields. Inline editing of 8 rows on a phone keyboard covers the list; a sheet keeps the bill visible behind.
